UK Government — Deep Dive

Constitutional monarchy + parliamentary democracy — how the pieces fit together

The Whole System at a Glance
The one-line summary The UK is a constitutional monarchy and a parliamentary democracy. Voters elect MPs to the Commons; the leader of the largest party becomes PM and forms a government. The monarch is head of state but does not run the government.

The Government — Who Does What

🏛️Prime Minister

  • Leader of the political party with most seats in the Commons
  • Appointed by the monarch — but in practice the role goes to the largest-party leader automatically
  • Heads the government — chooses Cabinet ministers
  • Lives and works at 10 Downing Street
  • Has a country residence at Chequers
  • Can resign or be replaced if loses party support

💼The Cabinet

  • ~20 senior ministers chosen by the PM
  • Each runs a government department (Treasury, Foreign Office, Health, etc.)
  • Meets weekly — takes the most important decisions
  • Notable cabinet roles: Chancellor of the Exchequer (finance, lives at 11 Downing Street), Home Secretary (immigration, police, security), Foreign Secretary, Defence Secretary

🎤The Speaker

  • Chairs debates in the House of Commons
  • Politically neutral once in the role
  • Elected by other MPs from among their own number
  • Responsible for keeping order — can suspend MPs
  • Decides which MPs speak; calls votes

📢Leader of the Opposition

  • Leads the second-largest Commons party
  • Heads the official Opposition in challenging government policy
  • Appoints a Shadow Cabinet to mirror government roles
  • Receives a salary specifically for this role
  • Faces the PM at Prime Minister's Questions (PMQs) — Wednesdays at noon
The Monarchy — The Crown's Role

👑King Charles III — Head of State (since 8 September 2022)

  • Reigns but does not rule. Constitutional monarch — symbolic, not political.
  • Opens new sessions of Parliament with the King's Speech (set by the government)
  • Signs every Act of Parliament into law — Royal Assent (last refusal: Queen Anne, 1708)
  • Officially appoints the Prime Minister
  • Head of the armed forces and the Church of England
  • Head of the Commonwealth (54 nations) and head of state of 14 other realms (Canada, Australia, NZ, Jamaica, etc.)
  • Heir apparent: Prince William (Prince of Wales since 9 September 2022)
Test fact The national anthem is 'God Save the King' (it was 'God Save the Queen' until September 2022). The royal house is the House of Windsor.
Devolution — Beyond Westminster

Scottish Parliament

At Holyrood, Edinburgh. 129 MSPs (Members of the Scottish Parliament). Elected every 5 years by a mixed system (constituency + regional list).

Powers: education, health, justice, transport, environment, much taxation.

Senedd Cymru / Welsh Parliament

In Cardiff. 60 Members of the Senedd (MSs). Originally the Welsh Assembly — renamed Senedd in 2020. Got primary law-making powers in 2011.

Powers: education, health, transport, environment, Welsh language.

Northern Ireland Assembly

At Stormont, Belfast. 90 Members (MLAs). Established by the 1998 Good Friday Agreement. Power-sharing executive between unionists and nationalists.

Powers: education, health, agriculture, justice (since 2010).

UK Parliament — Reserved Powers

Westminster keeps full control of: defence, foreign affairs, immigration, the constitution, most tax law, broadcasting, social security.

Devolution = transferred not given away — Parliament remains sovereign.
Easy mnemonic Holyrood — Senedd — Stormont: Scotland — Wales — Northern Ireland. England has no devolved parliament — it is governed directly by Westminster.
Elections & Voting
General electionEvery 5 years at most. Voters elect 1 MP per constituency. Largest-party leader becomes PM.
Voting systemFirst-past-the-post — the candidate with most votes in each constituency wins. (Devolved bodies use mixed systems.)
Voting age18 (16 in Scottish and Welsh elections)
Polling stationsOpen 7am to 10pm. Held in schools, halls, churches, etc.
Postal voteAvailable to anyone — no reason needed
Local electionsFor local councils — usually held in May
Police & Crime CommissionersElected for each police area in England and Wales
MayorsSome cities (London, Manchester, Liverpool) directly elect a mayor

Civil Service & Other Institutions

📋The Civil Service

  • Politically neutral — serves whichever government is in power
  • Implements government decisions; gives advice to ministers
  • Civil servants are not political appointees and don't change with the government
  • Standards: integrity, honesty, objectivity, impartiality

🇬🇧Local Government

  • County councils, district / borough / city councils
  • Responsible for: schools, social services, rubbish collection, planning, libraries, local roads
  • Funded by Council Tax (paid by residents) plus central government grants
  • Councillors elected by local residents

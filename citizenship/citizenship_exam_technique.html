Life in the UK Test — in summary

24 multiple-choice questions · 45 minutes · 75% pass mark (18/24) · £50 per attempt · sat at one of ~30 official UK test centres · based on the Home Office handbook "Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents".

A 6-Week Study Plan
1

Week 1 — Read the official handbook

Buy the Home Office book or read a summary. Don't try to memorise — just get the shape of it. Cover the five chapters once.

2

Week 2 — Values & History flashcards

Use the Values card and the History flashcards on this site. Test yourself on dates and the four patron saints. Aim to recognise without prompting.

3

Week 3 — Government & Law

Use the Government deep-dive and Law card. Memorise the names: Holyrood / Senedd / Stormont. Understand criminal vs civil. Get the court hierarchy clear.

4

Week 4 — Society & Culture

Society flashcards. Sport, religion, holidays, famous Britons. Many test questions are pure recall — this is where you bank easy marks.

5

Week 5 — Mock tests

Run the practice test on this site multiple times. Try other free mock tests online. Review every wrong answer immediately and re-read the relevant section.

6

Week 6 — Cram & book the test

Re-read the Top 25 facts (below) every day. Book the test for end of week 6 at gov.uk. The night before: light revision only and a good night's sleep.

Booking & On The Day
Where do I book?
Only at gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test — never use third-party sites. The official fee is £50. Book at least 3 days in advance.
What ID do I need?
Government-issued photo ID with your current address matching the booking. Acceptable: UK passport, BRP (Biometric Residence Permit), travel document, or photo driving licence. Bring two forms if possible.
What if I fail?
You can retake as many times as you like. You must wait at least 7 days between attempts and pay the £50 fee each time.
How long does the test result last?
A pass is valid indefinitely — you only need to pass once. Use it for ILR, then later for citizenship.
What does the test centre look like?
A small computer room with up to 12 candidates. Each on their own screen. No talking, no phones, no notes. ID checked at the door. Test runs immediately. Result printed on the spot.
Question Format & Common Traps

Do

  • Read each question fully — some say "Select TWO answers"
  • Watch for "TRUE / FALSE" questions — only two options
  • Re-read the question, not just the answers
  • Answer every question — no negative marking
  • If unsure, eliminate obvious wrong answers first
  • Use the "Flag for review" button and come back at the end
  • Watch the timer — ~110 seconds per question on average

Don't

  • Don't pick the longest-looking answer just because it's longest
  • Don't change a confident first answer without good reason
  • Don't get stuck on one hard question — flag it, move on
  • Don't confuse 1707 (Scotland in), 1801 (Ireland in), 1922 (Ireland out)
  • Don't confuse Scotland's 'Holyrood' with Northern Ireland's 'Stormont'
  • Don't assume "Republic of Ireland" is part of the UK — it's not
  • Don't pick "patriotism" or "loyalty to the King" as a fundamental British value — they aren't on the official list

Final Tips
✓

Take the official sample test before booking

The Home Office publishes free official sample questions on gov.uk. Score them honestly. If you get 18+ on three different mocks, you're ready.

✓

Don't trust unofficial "leaked" question lists

Many free sites copy slightly-wrong questions. The handbook is the only authoritative source. If a fact contradicts the handbook, the handbook wins.

✓

On test day — arrive 30 minutes early

You need time to find the building, present ID, store your phone in a locker, and settle in. If you're late, they may refuse entry and you forfeit the £50.

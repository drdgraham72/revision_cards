Values & Principles

The foundation of the citizenship test — what Britain stands for and what it asks of you

Why this matters

Two or three test questions are virtually guaranteed on this page

The Home Office test handbook opens with the values list and the citizenship pledge. Examiners use these as the simplest factual recall questions. If you only had ten minutes to revise, this is what you'd revise.

The 5 Fundamental British Values
1

Democracy

Government by the people, for the people. Decisions made by elected representatives accountable to voters.

In practice: regular free elections, MPs answer to constituents, no one is above the vote.
2

The Rule of Law

Everyone — citizens, ministers, even the monarch — is subject to the same laws. Laws apply equally and predictably.

In practice: independent judges, the right to a fair trial, no one punished without due process.
3

Individual Liberty

The freedom to live your life within the law — freedom of speech, religion, association, movement and choice.

In practice: you can criticise the government, follow any faith or none, and choose how to live.
4

Tolerance & Mutual Respect

Acceptance of those with different faiths and beliefs — even when you disagree. Britain is a multi-faith, multi-cultural society.

In practice: religious freedom is protected; discrimination on faith, race, sex or sexuality is illegal.
5

Participation in Community Life

Citizens are expected to contribute — volunteering, voting, jury service, helping neighbours, supporting good causes.

In practice: registering to vote, jury duty when called, volunteering or helping in your local community.
Exam tip Memorise these as "DRILT — Democracy, Rule of law, Individual liberty, Liberty (in religion/speech), Tolerance". Or simpler: "Democracy / Rule of Law / Liberty / Tolerance / Take part." A typical question is "Which of these is a fundamental British value?" with one obviously correct answer and three plausible distractors (e.g. "patriotism", "loyalty to the monarch") that are NOT on the official list.
The Citizenship Pledge

At the citizenship ceremony, you make this pledge to the United Kingdom. Two parts: an Oath of Allegiance (or Affirmation, if you don't want to swear by God) and the Pledge.

Oath of Allegiance

I, [name], swear by Almighty God that, on becoming a British citizen, I will be faithful and bear true allegiance to His Majesty King Charles III, His Heirs and Successors, according to law.

Pledge

I will give my loyalty to the United Kingdom and respect its rights and freedoms. I will uphold its democratic values. I will observe its laws faithfully and fulfil my duties and obligations as a British citizen.

Common trap The Oath is sworn to the Sovereign (currently King Charles III, since 8 September 2022). The Pledge is to the United Kingdom. Test questions sometimes swap these around.
Your Rights & Your Duties

What being a citizen entitles you to

  • Vote in all UK elections (local, devolved, general)
  • Stand for elected office
  • Hold a UK passport
  • Live and work in the UK indefinitely
  • Receive consular protection abroad
  • Equal treatment under the law, regardless of race, sex, religion, sexuality, age or disability

What being a citizen asks of you

  • Obey the law
  • Treat others with fairness and respect
  • Look after yourself and your family
  • Look after the area in which you live and the environment
  • Respect and obey the law — including others' beliefs
  • Serve on a jury when called
  • Vote in elections (a right, but a strong civic expectation)
Becoming a Permanent Resident — Key Facts
Test name
Life in the UK Test
Required for
Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) and British citizenship
Number of questions
24 multiple-choice
Time allowed
45 minutes
Pass mark
75% — 18 out of 24 correct
Fee
£50 per attempt
Booking
gov.uk — book at least 3 days in advance
ID required
Photo ID matching the address you booked with (passport / BRP / driving licence)
Language requirement
English (or Welsh / Scottish Gaelic) at B1 CEFR
Source material
Official Home Office handbook: Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents
Practical tip The test is taken on a computer at one of ~30 official test centres. You can retake it as many times as you want, but you pay £50 each time and must wait at least 7 days between attempts.
